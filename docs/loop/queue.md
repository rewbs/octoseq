| #   | Decision                                                                                                                                                                                                   | Why this default                                                                                                                                                                                                                          |
| --- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| D30 | A blob shadow's `color` is laid over the background by its opacity (ordinary alpha blending), so a bright colour at high opacity reads as a glowing pool rather than a shadow                              | The colour used to be discarded. ### Taken while scoring drum hits and sections

| #   | Decision | Why this default |
| --- | -------- | ---------------- |
| D44 | A drum sample's type comes from where the energy of its hits lies: kick if at least 80% is below 250 Hz, hat if at least half is above 4 kHz, snare if neither and it is centred above 500 Hz, otherwise no type. Claps and rimshots therefore count as snares, cymbals and shakers as hats, and toms as nothing | Set by looking at drum samples in the training modules whose names say what they are; the rule agrees with the name for 90% of them, and for every one of the 50 named kicks. Across the whole corpus it agrees for 88% of 324 named samples. The thresholds are constants in `modref.py`. The loop now checks the rule against sample names on every run |
| D45 | Drum hits are scored like onsets: a hit within 50 ms counts, a reference hit too quiet or too masked to matter is optional, and counts are pooled over modules. Analysers are compared on clean audio | The same conventions as the onset score, so the two can be read together |
| D46 | The detector has two sets of settings, one for the full mix and one for a separated drum stem, each found by `tune_drums.py` on the training split. The drum-stem set is searched on a third of the training modules | The two inputs want different thresholds. Separating every training module takes most of an hour |
| D47 | The drum-stem detector is scored on the clean audio of every third held-out module (39 of 117) | Demucs runs at about seven times real time on this laptop GPU, so every module in every variant would take hours. The rows say how many modules each figure rests on |
| D48 | Sections: a second signal-processing analyser, `change`, is registered alongside `novelty`. Chunk 1d is **not done**: the best analyser is still `novelty` at 0.520 against a target of 0.75. `change` led on the training split (0.58 to 0.54) and lost on held-out modules (0.496), so by the rules it is not an improvement and nothing uses it, and no model or hosted analyser is registered | Two further attempts failed. A boundary detector learned on the training split (logistic regression on spectral change) cross-validated at 0.48, below the hand-set rule's 0.60, and was dropped. The All-In-One structure model could not be installed: two of its dependencies (NATTEN and sphn) fail to build for Linux on ARM64. Music.ai's sections need its key (N2) |
| D49 | The section reference is left as it is, but it may be measuring the wrong thing. It puts a boundary wherever a fifth of the instruments change between patterns, which is one every 8 seconds on average (3.3 a minute) | That is finer than a structure model reports and finer than the director can use: a scene change every 8 seconds is too busy. A coarser second level (only the larger changes) would match both better. I have not changed the yardstick to make a number move; say if you want the coarser level added and scored alongside |
